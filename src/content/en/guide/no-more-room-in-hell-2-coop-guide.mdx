**Co-op is the entire point of No More Room in Hell 2: up to eight players share one huge map, spawning scattered at its edges and fighting to regroup toward the objectives at the center.** Getting a squad running takes just a few steps.

## Setting up a game with friends

1. **Create your session from the main menu.** Choose to host a server, then pick the map and scenario you want to run.
2. **Invite your friends.** Once the server is up, send invites through your friends list or share the session so teammates can join directly.
3. **Regroup in-game.** Players spawn at different spots on the map — use proximity voice chat to find each other, then push toward the objectives together.

Sessions support the full eight-player count, and since the 1.0 launch in August 2026, cross-platform play connects PC (Steam and Epic) with Xbox and PlayStation lobbies.

## How the co-op loop works

No More Room in Hell 2's structure differs from lobby shooters you may know:

- **You start alone, on purpose.** Every run drops you into the dark with next to nothing — the classic loadout is a single bottle — and the early game is about locating other survivors rather than shooting everything.
- **Proximity voice is your lifeline.** Voices carry based on distance, so calling out in the dark is both your best tool and a genuine risk. A party channel exists for coordinated teams.
- **Progress belongs to the squad.** The more responders who extract alive, the better the rewards — but if your character dies, that progress dies with them. Permadeath is the backbone of the co-op tension.
- **Scavenging feeds the team.** Weapons, ammo and medical supplies are scarce by design; sharing bullets with a teammate is often the difference between a full extraction and a wipe.

## Tips for a first squad run

Stay together once you've grouped up — splitting up to "cover more ground" is how teams feed the horde one player at a time. Share early-game weapons freely, since ammunition matters far more than the gun itself. And expect a slower pace than typical zombie shooters: methodical looting and planned routes beat sprint-and-spray every time.
