**No More Room in Hell 2 runs on Unreal Engine 5.** Torn Banner Studios listed UE5-powered visuals among the headline features of the game's early access release, and the engine switch is the single biggest technical difference from the original game.

## From Source to Unreal Engine 5

The first No More Room in Hell grew out of a Half-Life 2 mod and ran on Valve's Source engine. That heritage gave the original its distinctive map design — dense, hand-crafted environments that players still praise — but it also capped how far the visuals and systems could stretch.

Moving to Unreal Engine 5 for the sequel was a genuine reset for the small studio:

- Modern lighting and asset pipelines power the game's grim, cinematic look — from torch-lit farmhouses to the neon-soaked Broadway map added later in early access.
- The engine supports the sequel's much larger playable regions, where eight players spread across a huge map instead of fighting through one compact level.
- Tooling like Nanite and Lumen carries the zombie hordes, dismemberment and dynamic destruction that the original's engine struggled to scale.

## What that meant for development

The switch was not painless. The original's Source-based maps were a highlight precisely because the team had mastered that engine, and rebuilding that craft in Unreal took time — one reason the sequel spent close to two years in early access before its 1.0 debut in August 2026. Torn Banner has kept iterating on UE5's strengths since: the 1.0 "Armageddon" update and the Resupply (1.1) patch both pushed visual and performance improvements across every map.
