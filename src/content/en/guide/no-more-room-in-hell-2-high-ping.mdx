**No More Room in Hell 2 has no dedicated servers for its co-op sessions — connections are peer-to-peer, player to player.** That's the root of most high-ping complaints: your lag depends entirely on who you connect to and where they are.

## Why your ping is high

In a P2P game, one player's connection acts as the session's anchor. Match with someone on another continent and every action you take bounces across that distance before the game responds — no amount of local tuning removes that delay. During the game's rocky early access launch, this was compounded by a matchmaking bug that paired players across regions without waiting for same-region lobbies; Torn Banner's first hotfixes specifically made the matcher prioritise similar-ping players.

## What actually helps

1. **Play with players near you.** Team up with friends in your region, or join community groups for your area — same-region sessions are consistently smooth, cross-region ones aren't.
2. **Prioritise low-latency matchmakers.** Post-launch builds favour similar-ping matches; if you're seeing bad lobbies on a new build, re-check after the next patch.
3. **Close bandwidth hogs and network-blocking software.** Firewalls and antivirus suites can interfere with the game's network process; streaming, downloads and cloud backups running alongside a P2P session will spike everyone's ping.
4. **Use a wired connection.** P2P amplifies every jitter source — Wi-Fi drops that a dedicated-server game shrugs off will show up directly in your input delay.

## What doesn't help

No in-game setting can shorten the physical distance to your session host. Connection "boosters" only matter when your own route to other players is genuinely congested — for most players, region matching does more than any tool.

Cross-region play is inherently laggy in this game by design. If sessions keep failing to connect at all rather than just lagging, that's a different problem — see [server assignment failed](/guide/no-more-room-in-hell-2-server-assignment-failed).
