**Days after No More Room in Hell 2's rough early access debut, Torn Banner published a long reflection on X** — part apology, part repair plan. The post confirmed a second hotfix was underway for the following week, alongside details on the server improvements already implemented.

## The score was climbing — slowly

When the game launched, attention was high and reviews were brutal: roughly **34% positive** in the first days. The studio's first hotfix landed on October 24, addressing a slice of the launch problems. By October 26, SteamDB's tracking showed the score recovering to about **40.78%** — progress, but the player count told the other half of the story: concurrent players had slipped to around 1,300 as early adopters waited to see whether the fixes would hold.

## What the long post committed to

The statement read less like a victory lap and more like a public inventory of failures. Its core points:

- **Weekly hotfixes.** The team pledged a rolling cadence of patches, each targeting the worst remaining issues, and committed to sharing its plans openly as they evolved.
- **The November content update would shrink or slip.** Development capacity was being pulled toward fixes — the originally planned November update would be reduced or delayed as a result.
- **A public 1.0 promise.** The post's central quote: the team looked forward to advancing toward the full 1.0 vision, believing that putting the game in early access players' hands and improving it in development was the right path — so that when the full release arrived the following year, it could deliver on its promises.
- **An admission of the technical debt.** The studio acknowledged the game carried significant technical problems — bugs, performance, server stability — that had deeply affected the experience, and said these were its top focus.

## What came of it

The weekly patch rhythm held through late 2024, but the launch damage proved lasting: player counts kept sliding into December, when Torn Banner announced [layoffs alongside a renewed 1.0 commitment](/news/torn-banner-studios-layoffs). The road from that 40% score to the eventual 1.0 release in August 2026 took nearly two years — and the launch-week reflection is where that road was first mapped out.
