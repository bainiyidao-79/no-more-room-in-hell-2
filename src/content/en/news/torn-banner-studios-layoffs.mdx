**Torn Banner Studios announced layoffs in early December 2024**, barely six weeks after No More Room in Hell 2's troubled early access launch. The studio behind Chivalry had watched the co-op zombie shooter's player base collapse, and the cuts landed amid a brutal industry-wide layoff wave.

## How bad the numbers looked

Third-party SteamDB tracking painted a grim picture at the time:

- **Peak concurrent players: roughly 320**, with most sessions hovering in the low 200s.
- **Estimated sales: somewhere between 70,000 and 210,000 copies** based on player-held data — a wide band, but short of what a $29.99 multiplayer game needed to sustain a 150-person studio.

For comparison, the game had peaked at around 1,300 concurrent players during its launch week before the slide continued through November.

## What Steve Piggott said

CEO Steve Piggott framed the studio's position plainly: Torn Banner is a **fully independent, self-funded company**, and that made the challenges especially sharp. He committed to two things — affected employees would receive severance packages, and the studio would do its utmost to help them land new roles.

Critically, Piggott insisted No More Room in Hell 2 remained the studio's **core project**. The pledge: full effort toward shipping the full 1.0 version, continued work with the community, and delivering on the roadmap's promised updates and improvements. He repeated the 1.0-in-2025 target — a date the studio would later push to 2026.

## The wider context

The cuts did not happen in a vacuum. A PC Gamer report in the same 48-hour window catalogued an industry-wide purge: Ubisoft shut down XDefiant and its San Francisco and Osaka studios with around 277 roles affected, and Deceive Inc developer Sweet Bandits announced its own closure. Torn Banner — which had grown to more than 150 employees during the Chivalry years — announced a restructuring that cut staff it described as talented colleagues, with senior artists confirming the reductions were substantial.

The layoffs became the low point of the game's first year. The recovery — new maps, the delayed-but-delivered 1.0 "Armageddon" launch in August 2026 — is tracked in our [roadmap](/news/no-more-room-in-hell-2-roadmap) and [1.0 delay](/news/no-more-room-in-hell-2-1-0-delay) coverage.
